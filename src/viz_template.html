▶ Methodology & scope what this page does, and why

What you're looking at & why

Public companies make thousands of measurable, forward-looking promises in their annual 10-K filings — how much they'll invest, earn, or return to shareholders — yet almost no one checks afterward whether those promises were kept. This page closes that loop at scale: every bubble is one quantified commitment pulled from a filing, plotted as the amount promised (x) against what the company actually reported to the SEC (y) for that target year. The diagonal is a promise kept exactly; bubbles above it over-delivered, below it fell short. The goal is to make corporate promises auditable and comparable rather than forgotten.

1 · Finding promises

A rule-based pass (no LLM) scans the Business (Item 1) and MD&A (Item 7) sections of each 10-K. A sentence qualifies as a commitment when it pairs a corporate subject with a commissive verb — "we will / plan to / are committed to / target" — or states a hard deadline plus a quantified target.

Safe-harbor and pure-hedge boilerplate ("may", "could", "no assurance", forward-looking-statement disclaimers) is filtered out. Each candidate is scored on modality, deadline, target and first-person cues; only high-scoring sentences are kept.

2 · Judging "kept"

For a promise with a dollar (or %) target and a target year, the company's actual reported figure is pulled from SEC XBRL company facts for that fiscal year and compared as a ratio (actual ÷ promised).

Colour follows position on the chart: within ±15% of the line = kept, clearly above = exceeded, clearly below = missed. Ceiling / authorization promises ("up to $X", buyback limits) are excluded — they aren't a commitment to reach $X and their meaning inverts on this axis.

3 · Data sources

Filings: eloukas/edgar-corpus — 10-K item sections, 1993–2020.
Company names/tickers: SEC company_tickers.json.
Actuals: SEC XBRL company facts (data.sec.gov) — authoritative, free.

Concepts mapped: capex → PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment, revenue → Revenues, shareholder returns → dividends + repurchases, margin → OperatingIncomeLoss ÷ Revenues.

Scope & honest caveats

• The bubble chart plots only financially-verifiable promises — those with a dollar target and a matching reported actual. Climate pledges (net-zero, emissions, renewable, water) have no free per-company actuals in XBRL, so they can't be scored — instead they're shown in the Climate & net-zero pledges panel as commitments placed at their target year (outcomes still open).

• A verdict compares against the company-wide reported figure, which may be broader or narrower than the promise's scope (a single segment, a per-share amount). The verbatim sentence is shown on every bubble so you can judge the match yourself — treat verdicts as a screen, not a final judgment.

• Rule-based extraction trades some precision for transparency and yields occasional false positives. Implausible ratios (per-share figures, segment-vs-total mismatches) are dropped before plotting. This is an open, reproducible pipeline — not an audit.
